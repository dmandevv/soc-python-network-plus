Define different VLANs
- organizational, network engineering, security cameras
- you only have access to your VLAN
- can "hop" to another VLAN in 2 ways

1. Switch spoofing:
- takes advantage of a configuration on a switch that auto-configs an interface as either a "device" or "switch"
- if a switch sees another switch, it will send a "trunk negotiation"
- Switch spoofing works by a device pretending to be a switch
- this works because no authentication is required
- admins should disable trunk negotiation

2. Double tagging:
- craft a packet that includes two VLAN tags 
- takes advantage of the "native" VLAN configuration
- 1st "native" tag is read and removed by first switch, placed into the 1st VLAN - all is good
- 2nd "fake" tag is read and removed by second switch, packet has hopped into a VLAN they might not be allowed into
- this is a one way trip
- to solve, don't put devices on "native" VLAN
