# IEEE 802.3 (Ethernet) Standards

Naming convention: `[Speed][Signaling][Media type]` — e.g. **100BASE-TX** = 100 Mbps, Baseband signaling, Twisted-pair (X = specific encoding variant).

## Common standards

| Standard | Speed | Media | Max distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10BASE-T | 10 Mbps | Copper (Cat3+) | 100m |
| 100BASE-TX | 100 Mbps | Copper (Cat5+) | 100m |
| 1000BASE-T | 1 Gbps | Copper (Cat5e+) | 100m |
| 1000BASE-SX | 1 Gbps | Multimode fiber | ~550m |
| 1000BASE-LX | 1 Gbps | Single-mode fiber | ~5km |
| 10GBASE-T | 10 Gbps | Copper (Cat6a+) | 100m |
| 10GBASE-SR | 10 Gbps | Multimode fiber | ~400m |
| 10GBASE-LR | 10 Gbps | Single-mode fiber | ~10km |
| 40GBASE-SR4 | 40 Gbps | Multimode fiber (MPO) | ~150m |
| 100GBASE-SR10 | 100 Gbps | Multimode fiber | ~150m |

## Suffix / letter meanings

| Letter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| T | Twisted-pair copper |
| S (short) | Short-range multimode fiber |
| L (long) | Long-range single-mode fiber |
| X / R | Specific encoding scheme variants (e.g. SR = "Short Range") |
| CX | Short-range copper, typically twinaxial cable |
| CR | Copper direct attach cable (DAC), often twinax, short-range high-speed, common in data centers |

Copper always caps around 100m regardless of speed. Fiber varies widely: short-range multimode (hundreds of meters) vs long-range single-mode (kilometers).

## BASE = baseband

"BASE" signifies **baseband transmission** — the entire bandwidth of the medium carries a single data channel at a time (not divided into multiple simultaneous channels).

- Contrast: **broadband** divides bandwidth into multiple frequency channels via FDM (Frequency Division Multiplexing), letting multiple independent signals travel simultaneously (e.g. cable TV carrying dozens of channels + internet data over one coax cable, each on its own frequency slice; DSL splitting voice vs data frequencies on the same phone line).
- "GBASE" is used instead of "BASE" for gigabit+ standards (10GBASE-T, 40GBASE-SR4, 100GBASE-SR10).
- **Multimode fiber ≠ multiple channels.** "Multimode" refers to multiple physical light propagation paths/angles within the fiber core carrying the SAME single signal — not multiple independent data channels. This is actually why multimode has shorter max distance than single-mode (modal dispersion from the different path lengths smearing the signal). True multi-channel-over-one-fiber is a separate technology: WDM (Wavelength Division Multiplexing).

## Legacy coax standards (naming exception)

10BASE2 and 10BASE5 break the usual T/S/L/CX/CR letter-suffix convention — the number after "BASE" denotes approximate **maximum segment distance**, not cable type. That's why they needed common nicknames to actually communicate cable type.

| Standard | Nickname | Speed | Cable | Max segment length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10BASE5 | ThickNet | 10 Mbps | Thick coax (RG-8) | ~500m |
| 10BASE2 | ThinNet / CheaperNet | 10 Mbps | Thin coax (RG-58) | ~185m (rounded to "2" for naming) |

Both long obsolete, replaced by twisted-pair star-topology Ethernet — but a recognizable exam trap since they don't follow the standard suffix pattern.

## Twisted-pair cable categories

| Category | Max speed @ 100m | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cat 3 | 10 Mbps | Legacy, old telephone/10BASE-T |
| Cat 5 | 100 Mbps (2 pairs) | Can sometimes do 1 Gbps over all 4 pairs, not recommended/certified (higher crosstalk, no guarantee) |
| Cat 5e | 1 Gbps | "Enhanced" — tighter crosstalk (NEXT/FEXT) standards specifically to support all 4 pairs active simultaneously |
| Cat 6 | 1 Gbps @ 100m; 10 Gbps only at limited range (~33-55m) | Crosstalk performance not sufficient for full-range 10 Gbps |
| Cat 6a | 10 Gbps | "Augmented" — improved crosstalk performance for full 100m range at 10 Gbps |
| Cat 7 | 10 Gbps | Same speed ceiling as Cat 6a at 100m; advantage is better shielding (S/FTP, individually shielded pairs) and higher frequency support (600 MHz vs Cat 6a's 500 MHz) |
| Cat 8 | 25-40 Gbps | Short range only (~30m), designed for data center switch-to-switch/server connections |

## Other things 802.3 defines

- **CSMA/CD** (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection) — Ethernet's original shared-media access method. Largely obsolete now that switches eliminate collision domains, but still tested conceptually.
- **PoE (Power over Ethernet) standards** — deliver power over the same cable as data:
  - 802.3af (PoE): up to 15.4W
  - 802.3at (PoE+): up to 30W
  - 802.3bt (PoE++/4PPoE): up to 60-100W
