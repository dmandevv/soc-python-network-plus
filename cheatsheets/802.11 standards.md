# 802.11 Wi-Fi Standards

| Standard | Wi-Fi name | Year | Frequency band(s) | Max theoretical speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 802.11a | - | 1999 | 5 GHz | 54 Mbps | Less interference (5 GHz) but shorter range than 802.11b |
| 802.11b | - | 1999 | 2.4 GHz | 11 Mbps | Longer range, more prone to interference (shared with Bluetooth, microwaves, etc.) |
| 802.11g | - | 2003 | 2.4 GHz | 54 Mbps | Backwards compatible with 802.11b |
| 802.11n | Wi-Fi 4 | 2009 | 2.4 GHz & 5 GHz | 600 Mbps | Introduced MIMO (Multiple Input, Multiple Output) |
| 802.11ac | Wi-Fi 5 | 2013 | 5 GHz | ~6.9 Gbps | Introduced MU-MIMO (Multi-User MIMO), wider channels (up to 160 MHz), up to 8 spatial streams |
| 802.11ax | Wi-Fi 6 | 2019 | 2.4 GHz & 5 GHz | ~9.6 Gbps | Introduced OFDMA (better efficiency in dense/many-device environments) |
| 802.11ax | Wi-Fi 6E | 2020 | 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz & 6 GHz | ~9.6 Gbps | Same as Wi-Fi 6, extended into the 6 GHz band (less congested, more channels) |
| 802.11be | Wi-Fi 7 | 2024 | 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz & 6 GHz | ~46 Gbps | Introduces Multi-Link Operation (MLO) - can use multiple bands simultaneously |

## Key trends across generations
- Frequency expanded over time: 5 GHz only (a) → 2.4 GHz only (b/g) → both (n) → 5 GHz (ac) → all three bands (ax/6E, be)
- 2.4 GHz: longer range, more interference, fewer non-overlapping channels
- 5 GHz: shorter range, less interference, more channels/bandwidth
- 6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E+): least congested, most new channels, but shortest range
- MIMO (n) → MU-MIMO (ac) → OFDMA (ax) → MLO (be): each generation improves how multiple devices share the spectrum efficiently
